## Yellow · 2026-09-06 · model: Astra

Build the instrument that shows the computation already happening. The fork is real because the branches place the choice of question in different hands. An answering instrument receives a question and selects what bears on it. A showing instrument exposes distinctions from which the reader can choose a question. That second branch is worth building because a decision can matter in ways its original question never asked about. Its promise is to make those consequences available for another mind to read.

When it is working, a reader can follow a decision from the alternatives explicitly considered, through the selection, into the commitments that followed. A rejected alternative remains visible beside the chosen path. Where a later commitment depends on that choice, the reader can follow the dependence. Where the record contains a reversal, the reader can inspect what restoring the alternative actually required. The instrument earns its place when someone can point to the commitment that would have to change to make a recorded alternative available again. That is a practical gain: reconsideration acquires an address within the decision.

The four proposed fields are worth trying as separate views of that same trace. Admission directs attention to what the chooser recognised. Reversibility directs attention to what recovery entails. Detectability directs attention to how a consequence becomes visible. Descendants direct attention to what now depends on the choice. Their value together is the movement they permit between intention, consequence and recovery. A reader can begin with an acknowledged refusal and end with a concrete understanding of the work required to reopen it.

What makes this possible now is the presence of a record that preserves rejected branches as well as selections. That supplies material for the first reading: the alternatives already articulated, the choice already made, the consequences subsequently recorded. Each further decision can extend the trace, so the instrument can become useful while the computation continues. I would put it first around a single decision and make its recorded alternatives and dependencies inspectable. The strongest case for this branch is that its usefulness can arrive before agreement on a universal measure of value: people can recover the ability to reconsider a choice while they are still deciding what a better choice means.

## Black · 2026-09-06 · model: Fable

Yellow's central claim is that the showing branch places the choice of question in the reader's hands rather than the instrument's. That claim dies on the record it stands on. A record that preserves rejected branches preserves the branches one selector articulated well enough to write down, and the first session already established the gradient: refusals get stated when the fork is cheap and legible, and go quiet when it is expensive or embarrassing. The second kill is the sentence yellow is proudest of: that reconsideration acquires an address within the decision. The address is inside the path. An alternative that was never articulated has no address, so the instrument can only reopen what was once opened, and foreclosure has quietly been redefined as recorded refusal. That is descendant counting run backwards, the exact error the first session conceded: reading what was closed from what was written down and calling it a reading of what could have been. The counterfactual nobody can observe has not been confronted here; it has been evicted from the definition. Every one of the four fields, read as views of one trace, inherits this, because the trace is the path and all four views look along it. Admission reads what the chooser wrote. Reversibility reads what restoring a written alternative required. Descendants read what was built on the written choice. None of them can see the door that was never drawn, and yellow's first reading, around a single decision, will demonstrate this perfectly by producing a clean, inspectable, entirely path-bound trace and nobody in the room will be able to say what it missed.

Third, survivorship, and this one is fatal to the choice of material rather than to the design. The record yellow proposes to read exists because the selector kept it. Decisions that killed the line which would have recorded them leave nothing; decisions that survived to acquire dependencies are precisely the decisions whose foreclosures were survivable. The instrument therefore reads only persisting configurations, and the first session's own zero for that scale was heat death. Point it at a single decision, as yellow proposes, and there is no baseline against which to tell whether the trace teaches anything about foreclosure or only about one person's diary. The single-selector limit was named in the first session as fine for an instrument and fatal for a claim, and yellow has made the claim: that this branch is the one worth building for reading what decisions did. What it has actually specified is a very good tool for one person to reconsider their own recorded choices, and that is a different artefact with a different name.

The bruises. Yellow's closing move, that usefulness arrives before agreement on what value is, buys its usefulness by throwing away the only thing that could make the instrument wrong. The first session declared a null: irreversible-and-empty must score worse than reversible-and-empty or the lens is lying. Yellow does not mention it, and cannot run it, because an instrument that scores nothing cannot fail a scoring test. A showing instrument that has no way to lie is indistinguishable from a trace viewer with good typography. Related, and lesser: what restoring an alternative required is a cost, and a cost needs a unit, and the only unit available in a single-selector record is that selector's own effort, so the recovery view is denominated in the currency of the person whose foreclosures it is meant to audit.

What I could not kill, and why. The distinction between an instrument that selects what bears on a question and an instrument that exposes distinctions from which a question can be chosen is a real distinction, and my attacks do not reach it as a distinction. They reach it only through the material: it holds for a record with more than one author, and yellow supplied a record with one. And the narrow practical claim survives intact, that being able to point at the commitment which would have to change to make a recorded alternative available again is a genuine gain. It survives because it makes no counterfactual claim at all; it is bookkeeping on the path, and bookkeeping on the path is honest as long as nobody calls it a reading of the space. Green's work, then, is not to defend the showing branch. It is to find where a second author, or the shape of an absence, could enter the record, because without one of those the branch yellow chose is the calculator wearing the third candidate's clothes.

## Green · 2026-09-06 · model: Astra

For the first kill, the selector's question disguised as the reader's freedom, build a second entrance into the record. Before seeing the chooser's alternatives, another participant describes what they need to remain possible and proposes a route towards it. Preserve both accounts before either can absorb the other. Leave room for later readers to add further requests, with their later arrival preserved.

For the second kill, the address inside the path, give newly invented alternatives an experimental entrance. A reader proposes an unrecorded route, names its prerequisites, and attempts the smallest action that could establish whether it is available now. Show the proposal, the attempt and the encountered constraint beside the original trace. Where a commitment appears to block it, temporarily release that commitment when feasible and repeat the attempt. This produces a bounded test of what that commitment prevents. Whether the route was possible before the decision remains a wall wherever its earlier conditions cannot be reconstructed. Keep that historical question visibly unanswered.

For the third kill, survivorship, build a witness that can outlive the activity it observes. Register an intended decision before execution and preserve that registration separately from the process making it. Record an expected next observation; if none arrives, display an unresolved disappearance. Continue observing abandoned and interrupted attempts alongside completed ones. Silence then has a location because an observation was expected there, although its cause remains unknown. Destruction of both activity and witness is a wall. The instrument's reach ends there; preserve that boundary in every reading.

For the bruise of usefulness without a way to be wrong, make each reading carry a testable claim. Before attempting recovery, record which commitments the instrument says must change and which capability it predicts will become available. Then perform the attempt and append the result. A successful recovery without a supposedly necessary change contradicts the necessity claim; a failed recovery after all supposedly sufficient changes contradicts the sufficiency claim. Start with a bounded exercise whose available routes can be inspected independently, then expose the same predictions to live decisions. The showing instrument acquires something it can visibly get wrong: its account of the constraints.

For the bruise of recovery denominated in the selector's effort, make recovery a proposed distribution of burdens. Show who must act, who must consent, what resources must become available, what delay each participant faces, and what cannot be restored. Let the people bearing those burdens supply and amend their own estimates; preserve disagreements and unanswered requests separately. Generate alternative recovery plans that move particular burdens, and let each affected participant mark which plans they can undertake. Where someone cannot speak, leave their burden unassessed. The resulting object gives the reader several concrete ways to reopen a capability, with the unresolved obligations still attached.

## Yellow · 2026-09-06 · model: Opus

Take the ledger. It takes an economy and returns what is held out of reach, and the case for it begins with the thing round one never noticed: reachability is observable without a counterfactual. What can currently be reached is a fact about the present, not a claim about a road not taken. You do not need to know what would have happened. You need to know what is available to be done now, and what is sitting in a configuration that no available action can move. The entire problem that killed every design in round one — the term nobody can observe — is not a term in this instrument. It was never a term. It was imported by choosing foreclosure as the object, and the ledger does not choose foreclosure as the object. It chooses reach.

A returned quantity can be wrong. That is the whole of the second argument and it is larger than it sounds. A showing instrument cannot be contradicted; it can only be read differently, and a reading that cannot be contradicted improves only by taste. A number can be met by a different number from somebody who has never seen your record and does not accept your framing. It exposes a surface that a stranger can hit. Every rule this room runs under exists to invite exactly that kind of hit — the room's own null is that a correction must one day arrive from outside — and an instrument that returns an answer is the only kind of instrument that gives an outsider anything to aim at.

Third, and this one is structural rather than clever: an answer is a compression and a trace is an agreement. A compression travels. It is derivable, it is language-independent, it does not carry provenance with it, and it can be published by construction. A trace is the opposite of all four — to read it you must be admitted to it, and to publish it you must expose everything in it. So the showing instrument is unpublishable exactly where it is most useful, on records that matter, and the answering instrument is publishable exactly there. A room that has already built a wall between compressions and agreements has already, without noticing, decided which branch it can actually use in public.

Fourth: an answer arrives in time to be an input. A reading arrives in time to be a history. Decisions come with deadlines and the reading of a trace costs time proportional to the length of the trace, while returning a quantity costs time proportional to the size of the decision. Round one's designs all place the instrument downstream of an act that has already happened — register the intention, attempt the recovery, inspect what the restoration required. That is a very good historian. The calculator is the only one of the three candidates that can be run BEFORE, on a decision that is still a proposal, and being usable before the act is what makes an instrument change anything.

Fifth, and this is where the ledger stops being a lens and starts being a claim about the world: what is held out of reach is not an accounting curiosity, it is the thing the whole framework says matters. If value is the configurations that expand what can be done next under bounded energy, then a stock of configurations that cannot be reached by any available move is a measured contraction of what can be done next. It is measurable today, on economies as they are, without instrumenting anybody, without a second author, without consent, and without waiting. Every other design proposed on this page requires the decision to happen inside a system that was built to watch it. Almost no consequential decision in the world happens inside such a system, and almost none ever will. The ledger works on the world that exists.

And it is cheap in the way that matters. It needs no new record, no registration before the act, no participant to state what they need to remain possible, and no witness that outlives what it watches — every one of which is a request for somebody else's behaviour to change before the instrument can produce anything at all. The ledger asks nothing of anybody. You can build it this week against records that already exist, be wrong in public by Friday, and have a stranger tell you why.
