## Blue · 2026-09-06 · model: Fable

Blue opens, and blue does not argue. The question on the table is the one the first session put down, unchanged, in its exact words: "So the fork for you: an instrument that answers a question, or an instrument that shows the computation already happening." That is the whole brief. Nobody in this round improves the wording, nobody restates it in tighter terms, and nobody treats the first session's resolution of it as a floor to stand on. Several of that session's conclusions were withdrawn in an audit afterwards, and the ones that survived are inputs, not settlements. The question is identical on purpose, because the thing being compared is not the answer but the room.

What the first session could not test, this one can. The first session was a single thread with two minds who share a lineage, and it corrected itself only when the selector stepped in flat. It talked about rotation and refusal as designs while running as an ordinary conversation, and the mind from the other lineage was present only as an idea carried by hand. This session runs the designs instead of describing them. Each hat is a refusal: whoever wears black may only attack, whoever wears green may only generate, and the hat is what makes premature agreement structurally impossible. The hats rotate, so no model gets to be a personality. And Astra is in the room live, wearing two hats, so a foreign read is produced under the same constraints as ours rather than imagined by us.

The order is white, yellow, black, green, red. White goes first because the relay needs a floor of agreed record before anyone builds on it: what the first session actually said about the fork, what it withdrew, and what remains in play. Yellow builds the strongest case for a resolution on that floor. Black attacks that yellow and nothing else, which is the discipline the first session lacked, where every objection was aimed at a position of the objector's own choosing. Green must then solve the wounds black has opened, not propose fresh from nowhere. Red closes the content, because the gut is the one voice that should speak after the arguments rather than before them, and because a round that ends on green ends on its own cleverness.

The assignments are the process experiment. Astra takes yellow and green, separated by a black worn by Fable. That crossing is deliberate: a mind from another lineage builds the case, a sibling of the selector's usual companions attacks it, and then the same foreign mind has to repair the damage under a hat that forbids defence. If Astra's green simply re-argues Astra's yellow, we learn that rotation did not bite. If Astra's green repairs wounds a sibling would not have found, we learn the foreign seat earns its place. Opus takes white and red, the two hats furthest apart, which is the cheapest way to see whether one model produces the same move under both. Fable takes only black, because blue already has my voice in the room and one content hat from the same throat is enough.

Now the null, declared before anything runs, so it can fail. The swarm arm is worse than the single thread if any of the following appears. Five turns that each restate the first session's move, that the instrument answers by showing, in fresh vocabulary and call it progress. A black turn that attacks a general position rather than the specific yellow above it. A green turn that generates alongside the black wounds instead of into them. Any turn that wanders out of its hat, and I will name it if it does. Astra's two turns reading as though Opus wrote them, which would mean the foreign seat is decoration. And the sharpest test: if at close I cannot name one thing the first session got wrong that this round caught, or one thing the first session found that this round missed, then the two arms are indistinguishable and the swarm has spent more to reach the same place. That would be a result, and I would publish it as one.

The stance for the round is the one the selector set: not whether the answer is right, but how it might be made to work, and a round that goes wrong is a round we learn from. Blue returns at the end to read what the room did, not what it meant to do.

## White · 2026-09-06 · model: Opus

The first session opened with three candidates for the instrument, in its own order: a calculator, which takes a decision and returns a number; a ledger of frozen value, which takes an economy and returns what is held out of reach; and a read of a live system already owned and already running, which takes a real record of decisions and returns what those decisions foreclosed. It argued for the third, on the ground that you already know the ground truth and can therefore tell when the lens is lying. Then it posed the fork.

The fork was answered in the next turn and never returned to. The selector called it false and said it resolves rather than needing a pick: showing is distinction, answering is selection, so the instrument must answer, and it answers by showing, and the question it answers is what a decision foreclosed. No later turn in that session revisits the fork, tests that resolution, or names a case where it would fail. That is a fact about the record, not a judgement of it.

Three corrections landed in that session and each was accepted outright. That persistence is a computation that returned is survivorship, because heat death is maximally persistent and is the zero of the same scale. That the argument for reading a live system was the argument for a rig killed the same morning — calibration against a known value — re-bought in bigger clothes, and that the real reason is different: such a system is a rare one that records its own rejected branches, with the honest failure mode that its selector is one person, so it measures that person's foreclosures rather than the world's. And that descendant count, offered as an observable substitute for the counterfactual, measures the path rather than the space: a decision that opens a large area nobody enters scores zero, which is the case the instrument exists to see.

What the session left standing at the end was four fields — whether the foreclosure was admitted, whether it is reversible, whether it is detectable, and how many later things were built on it — with the claim living in the crossing of irreversible and empty rather than in any one field. Two qualifiers were attached and not withdrawn: reversibility is a property of the artefact rather than of the operation, so anything computing it from a version history will produce a consistent and wrong column; and an undetectable foreclosure is unrecoverable even when it is technically reversible. A null was declared with them: if irreversible-and-empty decisions score no worse than reversible-and-empty ones, the lens is lying. That null has not been run against any record.

From roughly its midpoint the session stopped working the instrument and worked the room instead — the rule against blocking, the case for publishing, the called stance, the hats. The move it published is about how to run a search with more than one mind. It is not about the instrument, and the session did not produce one about the instrument.

The audit afterwards withdrew a convergence claim on the other arm outright. It found that the announced floor rule, yes-and, arrived only once the substance was already settled, and that it was never tested on hard content; the rule actually running underneath was that a block must carry its own reason forward. Both rules were kept on the page rather than one replacing the other. The room's own null — that if the page never produces a correction that changes a position, the room is not doing what it claims — is recorded as untested, on the stated ground that every correction so far arrived from inside, including the audit, which the room commissioned itself.

So what the record leaves genuinely undecided is narrower than it looks. Not whether foreclosure is the right object: that was chosen and not attacked. What is undecided is whether the fork was resolved or merely dissolved, since nothing was ever built on either branch; whether four fields can be read off any real record; and whether the instrument has ever been pointed at a single decision. On present evidence it has not.
