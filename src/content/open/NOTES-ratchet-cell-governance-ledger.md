# The ratchet cell's governance ledger — what each level gives up, and where every level gains

_Published live (2026-11-09). A follow-on to the ratchet cell (`NOTES-ratchet-cell.md`). The question: as a cell climbs its ladder, what does it take from each level of governance, from community council to the UN — VAT, levies, network charges, business rates, grid upkeep? And which designs, already working somewhere, let every level gain more than it gives up? This is a desk pass by one agent: about a dozen sourced lookups, no fleet and no independent adversary seat. Each option carries its own adversary, but the run has not been through the gate. Figures marked ≈ are orders of magnitude, not models._

---

## The finding first: the loser is the roofless neighbour, not a level of government

Go through the ledger level by level and something unexpected appears. **Most levels of governance lose little or nothing to a ratchet cell, and several gain.** The real cost of the cell falls sideways, not upwards. It lands on the household with no roof, no share and no club place, which ends up carrying a larger share of the fixed system costs that cell members stop paying per kWh. The walls ledger in the parent note already lists this as *network-cost shifting: political contest*. This pass gives it its proper place. It is the only line in the ledger with a real loser, and that loser is not a government.

So "every level gains" is the wrong test, or at least only half of it. It mostly passes already. The test that bites is **every level gains *and* the non-participant is no worse off.** The options below are scored on both halves.

## The ledger — what a cell takes, level by level

The take comes in three kinds, and they should never be added together: a **fiscal loss** (money a government no longer receives), a **reallocation** (a revenue-capped cost that moves onto other bill-payers), and a **gain**.

| Level | What a cell touches | Kind | Size and sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Community council** (Cyngor Cymuned) | Nothing. The precept is unaffected. | — | Zero take. Possible gain: a council roof can host a rung-3 lease, and the council can hold or channel a benefit fund. |
| **County** (Gwynedd) | Non-domestic rates on standalone generation (hydro, ground-mount); planning. Rates are pooled nationally in Wales, so the county keeps nothing directly. | Fiscal, small | Rooftop and onsite plant has been **exempt from Welsh rates since 1 April 2024, to 31 March 2035** (Valuation for Rating (Plant and Machinery) (Wales) (Amendment) Regs 2023). The roof rungs therefore take nothing from rates. Only standalone assets (rung 7's hydro, any ground-mount) are rated. Gains: lower energy bills in its own schools and buildings, fewer fuel-poor households. |
| **Welsh Government** | The rates pool; Ynni Cymru grant spend (rung 0). Energy licensing is **reserved**, so there is no tax or levy lever here. | Spend in, target out | A net gainer against its own target of **1.5 GW locally owned by 2035**. Every cell is cheap delivery of a stated objective. |
| **UK Treasury** | VAT at 5% on every displaced kWh; 0% VAT on solar installs (to March 2027); income tax on share interest; CBS corporation tax; the share of the Renewables Obligation it now funds. | Fiscal loss, partly offset | VAT forgone ≈ **£25 a year per member household** displacing ~2,000 kWh at ~26p. Offsets: taxable share interest, employment taxes on the paid officer, and lower fuel-poverty spend. Probably net-negative but small. Not modelled. |
| **Ofgem / DNO** (SP Energy Networks, North Wales) | Distribution and transmission charges per kWh. | **Reallocation** | The DNO loses **nothing**: allowed revenue is set by the price control (RIIO-ED2), so lower volume raises unit tariffs for everyone else. The transmission and distribution *residuals* already moved to banded fixed charges under the 2022 Targeted Charging Review, so cells cannot dodge them. What cells still avoid is the per-kWh, cost-reflective part. The **gain** is flexibility: a cell battery or diverter that defers reinforcement is cheaper than copper, and the DNO keeps part of any underspend under its totex incentive. |
| **Policy levies** (RO, FiT, CfD, ECO, Warm Home Discount) | Per-kWh levies on supplied energy. | **Reallocation** | The total cost is fixed (certificates and contracts are owed whatever the volume), so a cell shifts levy onto non-members. **This contest shrank in April 2026.** The 2025 Budget ended ECO on bills and moved **75% of domestic RO cost to the Exchequer** for 2026–29 (≈£150 a year off the average bill combined; ≈£90 from RO alone, per the government factsheet). That moves cost from a regressive base (per kWh) to a progressive one (general taxation). It is the largest single cut to the cell's sideways take so far, and it happened for reasons unrelated to cells. |
| **EU** | Nothing. The UK is outside; ETS linking and CBAM do not reach a domestic cell. | — | Precedent library only (see options). |
| **UN** (UNFCCC, SDG 7) | Nothing. | — | Takes nothing and can give nothing a cell can spend. Its only currency is attributable mitigation in the UK's NDC, which cells add to at no cost to it. |

**Read it upward:** above the UK there is nothing to negotiate. **Read it at the UK:** the Treasury's loss is VAT-sized and small. The DNO's is zero by construction. **Read it sideways:** the real contest is levy and network reallocation onto non-members, and the 2026 levy move has already cut it by an unknown but material amount.

## The options

Each one is a design already running somewhere, mapped onto the cell's levels, scored on both halves of the test, and set against its strongest adversary.

### 1. Pay the backbone, sell the flexibility — the network level gains by buying what the cell can do

**Design.** The cell does not try to avoid network costs it causes. Members keep paying the fixed residuals (the 2022 Targeted Charging Review already makes that compulsory), and the cell volunteers a capacity-based contribution for any through-grid sharing (rung 5). The cell then *sells* to SP Energy Networks what it is physically good at: diverters and anchor batteries (rungs 1 and 6) bid into the DNO's flexibility tenders for the local substation, getting paid to defer reinforcement.

**Who gains.** DNO: deferral is cheaper than reinforcement, and it keeps part of the underspend. Non-members: lower reinforcement cost in their tariffs, and no residual shifted onto them. Cell: a new revenue line built on persistent value, which is exactly what condition 3 of the parent gate demanded ("price off self-consumption, avoided-network and flexibility value — the parts that persist").

**Precedents.** SPEN runs flexibility tenders across North Wales through Piclo/ElectronConnect: 113 MW secured at 12 HV/EHV sites in north-west England and North Wales in one earlier round. Italy's renewable energy communities (CER) reimburse only the *cost-reflective* network component on shared energy, not the residual. That is the right split. Germany's new energy sharing (§42c EnWG, live 1 June 2026) deliberately gives **no** network-charge reduction, on the grounds that shared electrons still use the wires.

**Adversary.** The tenders are thin and pitched at the wrong voltage. SPEN's 2025 round contracted **13.84 MW of 56 MW tendered**, mostly at HV/EHV. Low-voltage contracts, the voltage where a valley cell sits, are new and rare. Unless the Bethesda substation is actually constrained, there is nothing to sell, and the value per kWh may be pennies. Worse, Ofgem's *Energy System Cost Allocation and Recovery Review* (call for input closed September 2025) may push more cost onto standing charges, which cuts the unit-rate savings that rungs 2 and 5 run on. **Verdict:** passes both halves if the substation is constrained. Otherwise it is a posture with no revenue. One query to SPEN's constraint maps settles which.

### 2. A per-kWh royalty to the community council, reimbursed from above — the German §6 shape

**Design.** Large cell assets (the rung-7 hydro, any rung-3 anchor roof over a threshold) pay a small per-kWh royalty to the community council. The council uses its general power of competence to spend it on the fuel-poor cohort the parent gate found the ladder cannot reach (the "false for the acute fuel-poverty cohort" clause). The decisive feature is borrowed exactly: **the royalty is reimbursed by the level above**, so the asset owner is neutral, the community council gains, and the payer is the level that wants consent and build rate.

**Precedents.** Germany, §6 EEG: operators may pay affected municipalities up to **0.2 ct/kWh**, and for EEG-subsidised plants the grid operator reimburses it. In effect the national surcharge account pays the municipality. Since the 2024 EEErtrBetG, projects approved after 31 December 2024 must pay (0.2 ct/kWh wind, 0.1 ct/kWh ground-mount PV, up to 0.4 ct/kWh for wind by agreement). The UK has now built its own version for consent: the **Bill Discount Scheme** gives households within 500 m of 43 new transmission projects £250 a year for ten years from 2027, funded by every bill-payer at roughly 80p–£2 a year. Wales keeps a register of community benefit (the £5k/MW onshore wind norm).

**Who gains.** Community council: income it has never had, ring-fenced to the cohort the ladder fails. County and Welsh Government: progress on the fuel-poverty and local-ownership targets. Nation: social licence. Non-members: if the royalty pays for fuel-poverty outreach, the most exposed non-participants are the ones who gain.

**Adversary.** The UK has no EEG account. If nobody above reimburses, the royalty is just a local tax on a thin-margin rung, and the capital layer (condition 1, possibly clearing only 2–3%) cannot carry it. Scale is honest but small: 0.2p × a 100 kW hydro's ≈400 MWh is ≈**£800 a year**. That is a real outreach budget for one valley, but no more. And the community council is itself run by unpaid volunteers, so the parent note's "residual saint" moves rather than disappears. **Verdict:** passes both halves *only with* an upstream reimburser. The candidate is Great British Energy's community fund or Ynni Cymru, paying the royalty rather than the capex. Without one it fails the asset-owner half.

### 3. Exempt the community, retain the commercial — the county finally holds a stake

**Design.** Split the rates treatment of standalone generation by ownership. Community-owned assets (CBS, asset-locked) get full relief. Commercial generation in the same county pays rates, and the county **retains** them instead of pooling them. Gwynedd gains real income from the big commercial projects it hosts, the cell's rung-7 assets pay nothing, and the Welsh pool loses only the small rates bill on community assets.

**Precedents, one per nation.** *Scotland:* Renewable Energy Generation Relief gives community-owned schemes (≥15% of annual profit to a community organisation) 100% relief up to £145k rateable value, tapering above. *England:* the Non-Domestic Rating (Renewable Energy Projects) Regulations 2013 let councils keep rates from designated renewable projects, protected from the April 2026 retention reset. *Wales:* onsite plant has been exempt since April 2024, so the roof half is already done. *Spain:* municipalities may voluntarily waive up to **50% of IBI** (property tax) and **95% of ICIO** (works tax) on solar installs. Many do, so the lowest level gives up tax by choice, because the local return is worth more to it.

**Who gains.** County: a retained income it lacks today. Welsh Government: delivery of the 1.5 GW target at the cost of a small slice of the pool. Cell: cheaper rung-7 assets. Non-members: neutral to slightly positive (council income).

**Adversary.** Wales pools non-domestic rates *on purpose*: it is redistributive. Letting resource-rich counties keep generation rates takes from the counties without wind or water. That is zero-sum inside Wales, which is the very move the option claims to avoid. England's retention mostly produced nothing, because the de facto onshore wind ban followed straight after. And a small community hydro may already sit under small business rates relief, so the relief half could be worth zero. The Spanish evidence cuts both ways: 38% of municipalities offer no IBI bonus at all. **Verdict:** passes for the county and the cell. Fails the "every level" test at Wales level unless retention is limited to *new* commercial capacity above a baseline, which is how England designed it. Needs the rateable value of the Ogwen hydro to price the relief.

### 4. Move the take from the bill to the tax base — the Dutch fiscal route, and why it was abandoned

**Design.** Close the sideways contest at the root. The cost a cell shifts onto non-members is whatever is recovered *per kWh* without being caused per kWh: levies and network residuals. Every pound moved from that base to general taxation shrinks the cell's take from its roofless neighbour, and a tax base is progressive where a per-kWh charge is regressive. The cell-specific version adds a published **fiscal ledger** for each cell: VAT forgone set against taxes paid (share interest, wages, CBS corporation tax) and fuel-poverty spend avoided, so the Treasury can see its own balance.

**Precedents.** *UK, already moving:* the 2025 Budget took ECO off bills and 75% of domestic RO cost into the Exchequer for 2026–29, under the Energy Independence Bill. *Netherlands:* the **postcoderoosregeling** (to March 2021) gave cooperative members in neighbouring postcodes a reduced energy-tax rate on their share of the cooperative's output. It was a direct Treasury-to-cell fiscal transfer. It was **replaced by a subsidy** (the SCE, from April 2021) because the fiscal route was complex and hard to target, and the first SCE round was fully allocated in about two months, which is waitlist evidence. *Belgium (Flanders):* the meter-that-runs-backwards was struck down in 2021 precisely as a regressive cross-subsidy. That is the counter-precedent this option exists to avoid.

**Who gains.** Non-members: most of all, because the shift onto them shrinks. Treasury: neutral-to-negative, but now *visible*, and the per-cell ledger turns an argument into arithmetic. Cell: unaffected or slightly negative (smaller per-kWh savings), which is exactly the spread compression the parent template already designs around.

**Adversary.** This option is mostly a description of what is already happening, plus a ledger. The RO transfer is time-limited (to 2028–29), and a future budget can reverse it. The Dutch history says tax-route support for cells gets converted into subsidy once it grows, and subsidy is the grant-dependence the ladder is built to avoid ("no later rung's rationale cites grants"). The fiscal ledger is unvalidated, and an honest one may show a net Treasury loss. **Verdict:** the strongest on the non-member half and the weakest on the Treasury half. Its real value is defensive: it puts a number on the "regressive cells" attack before someone else does.

### 5. The frame-breaker: share the electrons without sharing the wires

**Design.** Stop asking how to compensate the levels the cell takes from, and push value down the ladder towards rungs that take nothing. Behind-the-meter and private-wire rungs (1, 2, 3 and anchor batteries) use no public network for the shared kWh, so they shift no network cost at all. Through-grid sharing (rung 5) is where the reallocation lives. Reorder the cell's growth plan so its expansion comes mainly from roofs, diverters, private wires and behind-the-meter storage, and treat through-grid matching as the *top-up* layer instead of the core.

**Precedent.** The physics, and the German legislator's reading of it: §42c gives through-grid sharing no network relief *because* it uses the network. Italy, Spain and Austria went the other way and discount shared kWh, which buys uptake at the cost of the sideways shift. Bethesda's own evidence points the same way: the binding constraint is *generation supply*, not demand. The missing rungs are roofs, which are behind the meter by design.

**Who gains.** Every level, almost trivially, because almost nothing is taken. Non-members: not harmed by behind-the-meter self-consumption beyond its levy share, which the 2026 move has shrunk.

**Adversary.** Self-consumption is still volume that does not pay per-kWh levies, so the sideways shift shrinks but never reaches zero. More seriously, behind-the-meter value goes only to people with roofs, private wires or anchor hosts, which is exactly the equity residue the parent gate found. Rung 5 is how a roofless member joins. Shrinking it to protect roofless non-members excludes roofless would-be members. **Verdict:** the cleanest answer to the governance question and the worst answer to the equity question. Its job is as a dial, not a doctrine: grow behind the meter by default, and keep through-grid membership open as the roofless door, paid for under option 1.

## What I think

The question assumed the fight was vertical: parish against county against Westminster. The ledger says it is horizontal. Above the UK nothing is taken. At the UK level the Treasury's loss is VAT-sized and the DNO's is zero by design. The real cost lands on non-participating bill-payers, and it is already shrinking for reasons unrelated to cells (the 2022 residual reform, the 2026 levy move). **My read: option 1 plus option 5 is the ratchet cell's governance posture by default.** Grow behind the meter, pay the backbone, sell flexibility where the substation is constrained. That costs nothing to adopt and passes both halves wherever a constraint exists. **Option 2 is the one worth campaigning for**, because it is the only design that turns a level of government into the *funder* of the fuel-poverty cohort the ladder cannot reach. It needs exactly one thing the UK lacks, an upstream reimburser, and GB Energy's community programme is the obvious candidate. Option 3 is good for Gwynedd but breaks the Welsh pool, so it only works in its English, new-capacity-only form. Option 4 is a defensive ledger to keep in a drawer, not a strategy.

## What I would do next

1. **One lookup that decides option 1:** SPEN's network capacity and constraint map for the Bethesda primary substation. Constrained means a flexibility line exists. Unconstrained means option 1 is posture only.
