# The Lawrence Weston dossier: the bridge was built for four months

_Published live (2026-11-09). One of seven mid-climb dossiers; the survey and ranking are in `NOTES-mid-climb-survey.md`. This is the survey's urban case and its fuel-poverty case. **Process honesty:** desk artefact, single-seat, no adversary. Tags: **[verified]** means fetched live on 2026-11-09 with the source named; **[desk]** means inference._

## The sharpest objection, on the face

**Lawrence Weston has already crossed the river once and came back.** OVO ran a "Community Power Project" for the estate. It was a 4-month trial from 2 April 2024 to about 2 August 2024. It was capped at the first 300 sign-ups and open only to OVO customers in BS11 with a communicating smart meter. They got at least 15p credit for each kWh used on "Windy Days" **[verified — OVO trial terms]**. The search found no sign that it was renewed **[desk — absence of evidence, not a confirmed ending]**. If a large supplier's trial did not become a standing offer, the reason may be the same one that will defeat a club. Whatever that reason is, it is the real finding about this place, and it sits on the off-desk list, not in the desk's guesses.

## The estate as found

Lawrence Weston, north-west Bristol (BS11): a post-war estate, and the community that led its own regeneration.

- **The organ.** **Ambition Lawrence Weston (ALW)**, a community regeneration charity working to a Lawrence Weston Community & Climate Plan **[verified — Wikipedia; West of England CA case study]**.
- **The generator.** **Ambition Community Energy CIC** owns a 4.2 MW, 150 m turbine, described as England's tallest or largest onshore turbine. Construction began in July 2022 after seven years in development, with finance from Thrive Renewables. It is "100% community-owned". Its power is "sold into the national grid and all profit returned" to ALW, at a projected £100k or more a year **[verified — Thrive; colour-of-money.co.uk, Jun 2026; Wikipedia]**.
- **National recognition.** The government's Local Power Plan (February 2026) names the ACE turbine as an example of community energy **[verified — Thrive Renewables, Feb 2026]**.
- **Neighbouring generation.** Bristol Energy Cooperative built a 4.2 MWp solar farm in the Lawrence Weston area in 2016, and that farm led to ACE being set up **[verified — Community Energy England case study]**.
- **What is absent:** a standing local supply arrangement. No Energy Local club covers Bristol. The nearest club is Fairy Hill, south of the city **[verified — energylocal.org.uk/clubs]**.

## Distance to the river

**Near in plumbing, and the place where the bridge test's equity residue is sharpest.** A supplier has already shown it can identify the households, read them half-hourly and pay them for using local wind. So the metering and billing path has been tried here, live. What failed, or lapsed, was the offer's reach and how long it lasted: a supplier lock-in (OVO customers only), a cap of 300, and four months. A club with a licensed partner removes the lock-in. The ratchet cell's verdict said the median adopter crosses alone and the fuel-poor cohort needs funded outreach. Here, ALW is that funded outreach, and it is paid for by the turbine itself.
