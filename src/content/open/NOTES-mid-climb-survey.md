# The mid-climb survey: seven places with live community energy and no club

_Published live (2026-11-09). This survey follows the Nantlle and Corris dossiers. It looks for places partway up the ratchet-cell ladder: they own generation and an organ to run it, but no local supply club has formed yet. Five are in the UK and two are abroad. Each place has its own short dossier. This page ranks them by **distance to the river**. In the bridge-test parable, the river is the gap between a community that generates power and its own households using that power. A match club is the bridge. **Process honesty:** desk work, single-seat, no adversary. The fleet got Nantlle wrong before, so every place was checked live on 2026-11-09 against its own sources and the full Energy Local club list. Three first-pass candidates were dropped because a club already covers them: Talybont-on-Usk (inside the Brecon club), Settle (it has its own club), and Tiree (it has a club, which turned out to be the most useful fact in the Gigha dossier)._

## Closest to the river: Awel Aman Tawe

**My read: Awel Aman Tawe.** It is the only place where every asset upstream of the match club is already standing or being built, and the missing rung is a supply contract, not a founding act. The generation is far larger than any club needs. Egni is the national example of the free-roof anchor. The organ has paid staff and an advice team already at households' doors. Its members have bought shares twice. The battery being built at Gwrhyd raises the match percentage just as the question can be asked again. Templederry might be closer, because one contract between two community bodies in the same county could do it. But that depends on a PPA end date and an Irish sharing rule that this desk could not verify, so it ranks second on the evidence and not on the hope.

**The honest weight against it is the nine-year question again.** Awel has turned since January 2017 with Energy Local clubs operating in neighbouring South Wales counties. As Llanberis taught, nine years next to a working mechanism reads as *declined*, not *unclimbed*. So the claim that Awel is closest stands only if the reason it declined has changed. The battery is a candidate reason, and the question can be put to one board in one meeting. Off-desk item 1 of the Awel dossier is that meeting.

## What the survey shows across the seven

- **Two different kinds of distance.** Institutional distance means how many founding acts are left. Physical distance means whether there is matched generation at all. Torrs is short on the first and long on the second, Fintry the reverse, and Awel is short on both.
- **The clocks are contracts.** Four of the seven have a dated event coming: ROC end (Fintry), PPA end (Templederry), repowering (Gigha, Fintry) and a battery commissioning (Awel). Each one is the cheap moment to write a local-supply line into a contract. This is a fifth observation of `the-closing-window`'s counterparty-rebuild clock type.
- **The "impossible" claim is a stale ledger.** Gigha says local use is impossible while Tiree's club runs. The house rule from the audio estate applies here too: test the live path before believing what the record says.
- **Abroad sharpens the walls.** Germany legalised the bridge and found it priced out, which puts it in the political-contest wall from the ratchet cell's ledger. Ireland has the community supplier GB lacks but not the sharing rung. Neither undoes the template. Both show which parts of it depend on GB-specific regulation.
