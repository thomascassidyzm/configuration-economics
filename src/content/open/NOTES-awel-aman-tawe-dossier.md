# The Awel Aman Tawe dossier: the whole stack, no club

_Published live (2026-11-09). One of seven mid-climb dossiers (the survey is `NOTES-mid-climb-survey.md`). The places in this survey have live community energy and no local supply club yet. **Process honesty:** desk artefact, single-seat, no adversary has run on it. ## The sharpest objection, on the face

**Awel has turned beside a working club pattern for nine years without building one, and the survey has already read that shape once as *declined*, not *unclimbed*.** The two turbines have run since January 2017 **[verified — aat.cymru/awel]**. Energy Local clubs run in neighbouring South Wales counties: Brecon, Crickhowell and Llanymddyfri **[verified — energylocal.org.uk/clubs]**. AAT is one of the most capable community energy bodies in Britain. If no club exists here, the likeliest reason is that a club priced worse for AAT than selling 12 GWh a year to the grid. It is not that nobody thought of it. This is the Llanberis nine-year question again, and this dossier cannot answer it from the desk. Off-desk item 1 is the whole case.

## The valley as found

The upper Amman and Swansea valleys, about 20 miles north of Swansea. The site is a former mining district.

- **The organ.** **Awel Aman Tawe**, a community energy charity. Its staff live in the local villages **[verified — aat.cymru]**.
- **Wind.** **Awel Co-op** owns two Enercon turbines of 2.35 MW each (4.7 MW) on Mynydd y Gwrhyd. They have been running since 25 January 2017 and make over 12,000 MWh a year. The project was financed with £3m of community shares and a £5.25m Triodos loan. Interest has been paid to members every year **[verified — aat.cymru/awel, aat.cymru/egni]**.
- **Solar on roofs.** **Egni Co-op** was founded in 2013 as a pilot on 7 community buildings. It now has nearly 5 MWp across 90+ sites and calls itself the UK's largest rooftop solar co-op. Its shape is the free-roof anchor: it sells on-site power to hosts at 18–23p and exports at 8.87p (2025 figures). In June 2026 the Development Bank of Wales lent it almost £1.4m for about 2 MW more **[verified — aat.cymru/egni; APSE Egni slides]**.
- **Solar plus battery, under construction now.** **Gwrhyd Solar Farm** (Haul y Gwrhyd CIC) is a 2.6 MWp "tribrid" with a 0.9 MW / 5 MWh battery, sharing the wind farm's grid connection. Construction started on 21 September 2026 **[verified — aat.cymru news, 3 Nov 2026]**. Summer solar fills the wind's seasonal trough, which is rung 7's winter-mutual logic run in reverse.
- **Second commodity, already running.** **Taith** is a community electric transport scheme with three EVs and five bikes; an EV car club is planned. **Hwb y Gors**, a bought former school, houses the Energy Advice, Community Transport, and Arts and Education teams **[verified — aat.cymru; climate.cymru, 14 Jul 2026]**.
- **Stated intent.** AAT has written approvingly of the national aim to "enable local supply so that communities can sell the energy they generate to local people" **[verified — aat.cymru, 'Good news for community energy', 11 Jul 2024]**. That shows an aim, not a project.
- **What is absent:** a club. None of the 40 Energy Local clubs covers Cwmllynfell, Gwaun-Cae-Gurwen, Brynaman or Ystalyfera **[verified — energylocal.org.uk/clubs; one listed club, "Bro Rhian", could not be located, so it is flagged rather than excluded]**.

## Distance to the river

**One rung, and nothing upstream of it is missing.** Every asset a club needs is standing or being built: generation far larger than any club's demand, a battery, an organ with paid staff, an advice team already at households' doors, and a member base that has bought shares twice. The match club is the only rung missing, and it is a supply contract, not a founding act. The battery arriving in 2026–27 makes the match percentage better than it would have been at any point in the last nine years. That gives an honest reason why the answer to "why not before?" might now change.

## Off-desk list

1. **Ask AAT the nine-year question:** has a club been priced to the Awel board? If it was declined, record why, word for word. (Was it a PPA lock-in, the supplier partner, or the economics at 12 GWh?)
2. **Awel's PPA:** who buys the power, on what term, and when the term ends. A club can only take power the PPA allows to be carved out.
3. **Which grid supply point Mynydd y Gwrhyd sits on**, and which villages share it. A club's catchment is set by the network, not by the parish.
4. **The tribrid's operating plan:** is the battery merchant-traded, or could it serve a match pool?
5. **Bro Rhian:** locate the club and confirm it does not cover the Amman valley.
