# The Torrs Hydro dossier: rung 3 since 2008, and a river that ran dry

_Published live (2026-11-09). One of seven mid-climb dossiers; the survey and ranking are in `NOTES-mid-climb-survey.md`. **Process honesty:** desk artefact, single-seat, no adversary. Tags: **[verified]** means fetched live on 2026-11-09 with the source named; **[desk]** means inference._

## The sharpest objection, on the face

**The generator is too small and, in 2026, too dry to feed a club.** Torrs made 191,228 kWh in 2024, 97,764 in 2025 and 105,576 in 2026 to 23 September. Low river levels from April 2026 stopped the turbine by early July **[verified — torrshydro.org]**. Even its best year is about the annual use of 70 homes **[desk arithmetic, ~2,700 kWh per home]**, and a nearby Co-op store already takes the power first. A club built on this generator would mostly sell members the top-up supplier's power. On its own generation, New Mills is a weak candidate, and saying so is the point of this dossier.

## The town as found

New Mills, Derbyshire: the Goyt valley, at the Torr gorge.

- **The generator.** "Archie" is a reverse Archimedes screw with a maximum rating of 63 kW, at the confluence of the Sett and the Goyt. It has generated since summer 2008, with 2.2 GWh in its lifetime **[verified — torrshydro.org; uk.coop directory]**.
- **The wrapper.** Torrs Hydro New Mills Ltd is a Community Benefit Society incorporated in 2007 and run entirely by volunteers **[verified — torrshydro.org]**. Shareholders may give their interest to a community fund **[verified — search of project pages; Wikipedia]**.
- **Rung 3, standing for 18 years.** The power goes directly to the nearby Co-op supermarket and the surplus goes to the grid **[verified — torrshydro.org]**. This is the free-roof-anchor shape, a private sale to one daytime-loaded host, running since 2008.
- **The volunteer residue, visible.** During the 2026 drought the volunteers reconditioned the generator and replaced the bottom bearing **[verified — torrshydro.org]**. This is ratchet-cell condition 2 (the unpaid board carrying fiduciary liability) seen in practice.
- **What is absent:** a club. No Energy Local club covers the High Peak **[verified — energylocal.org.uk/clubs]**.

## Distance to the river

**Far on generation, near on precedent.** The organisational distance is short: a CBS exists, a private-wire host sale has worked for 18 years, and the community has funded the scheme twice. The physical distance is the problem. Any club would need new generation, most plausibly rooftop solar on mills and civic roofs, which would also cover the hydro's summer drought. Solar and run-of-river are seasonal opposites, and the second-cell treatment's winter-mutual logic applies here too. The survey learns something from New Mills: the river can be near for the institution and far for the physics.

## Off-desk list

1. **The surplus after the Co-op takes its share.** If almost nothing is exported, a club has nothing to match.
2. **Roof census:** the mills, schools and churches within the substation's catchment.
3. **Volunteer load:** how many people carry the CBS, and whether a club would add work to the same people.
4. **The drought:** whether the 2026 stoppage is a one-off or a trend in the Goyt's flows.
