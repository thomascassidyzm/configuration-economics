## The sharpest objection, on the face

**The island's own trust says local use is impossible, and it has been running the turbines for twenty-one years — it knows more than this desk.** The Windfarm page: both companies "sell all their power to energy buyers. Using the energy on the island is a future aspiration, but current legislation and costs currently make it impossible" **[verified — gigha.org.uk/Windfarm]**. Against it stands one fact: **Tiree** — another Argyll island with a community turbine — has a live Energy Local club **[verified — energylocal.org.uk/clubs]**. Either the Gigha statement is stale or there is a Gigha-specific reason (grid, meter estate, supplier) the desk cannot see. The dossier rests on that gap and cannot close it from here.

## The island as found

- **The trust.** Isle of Gigha Heritage Trust — the 2002 community buyout **[desk — background, not re-verified]**.
- **Generation.** Three second-hand Vestas V27s ("the Dancing Ladies," installed 2004, run by Gigha Renewable Energy Ltd) plus an Enercon E33 (producing from October 2013, run by Gigha Green Power Ltd); both pass profits to the trust **[verified — gigha.org.uk/Windfarm]**.
- **The age clock.** A 2026 member memo: the V27s have run on Gigha 21 years but are 33 years old; repowering options discussed at community meetings on 16 April 2026; grant funding "likely to be proportionately less than previously." The board chose continued maintenance (January 2026 quotes £55k–£75k) because "funding is not available for more ambitious options at present" **[search-summary — forum.gigha.org.uk member memo and meeting record, 2026]**. An earlier options appraisal by Vestas included **privately supplying a local business** **[search-summary]**.
- **What is absent:** a club, and on the trust's own account, any local use.

## Distance to the river

**Close in substrate, far in will — and the repowering decision is the hinge.** Everything a club needs is on one small grid island under one owner: generation, a community body, a bounded population. What is missing is a belief that it is legal and affordable, and Tiree's club is the live existence proof that the belief can change. The repowering is the clock: a new turbine sized and contracted *with* a club offtake in the design costs nothing extra to specify, while retrofitting a club onto a fresh 20-year PPA does. The private-supply-to-a-local-business option the appraisal already looked at is rung 3 (free-roof anchor, private wire) — the cheapest step and already on their table.
