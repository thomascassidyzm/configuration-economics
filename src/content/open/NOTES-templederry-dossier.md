## The sharpest objection, on the face

**Ireland is not the UK, so the ladder's rung 5 may not exist here at all.** The ratchet cell's match-club rung stands on GB exemptions and the Energy Local supplier-partner model. Ireland has no direct equivalent that this desk could verify. A 2026 Horizon Europe pilot (INNO-TREC, with the Dingle Hub) is still *testing* community sharing in Ireland **[verified — EPRI Europe, 13 Jan 2026]**. If the rung is not legal or not priced here, Templederry is not "mid-climb". It is at the top of a shorter ladder. The dossier is still worth having because of a different crossing: a community-owned *supplier*.

## The parish as found

Templederry, north Tipperary, near Nenagh.

- **The generator.** **Templederry Wind Farm Ltd**: 2 turbines, 4.6 MW, at Lisgarriff West. It began as a 1999 feasibility idea, connected in November 2012 and opened in September 2013. It is Ireland's first community-owned wind farm, and two shares are held by a community co-operative for the wider community **[verified — Interreg Europe good practice; merrionstreet.ie; Irish Times]**. It produces about 15 GWh a year **[dated — Social Finance Foundation, Aug 2022]**.
- **The singularity.** About seven years after opening, it was still Ireland's only community wind farm in operation. Its co-founder blamed a system "geared against community generation projects" **[dated — Irish Examiner, c.2020]**.
- **The supply deal.** A 15-year supply agreement with Bord Gáis **[dated — SFF, Aug 2022]**. If it started at connection in late 2012, it ends around **late 2027** **[desk arithmetic; the start date is not verified]**.
- **The community supplier, next door.** **Community Power**, based in Nenagh, calls itself "Ireland's FIRST Community Owned Renewable Electricity Utility Company". It offers domestic and business tariffs, and its site announces a price change from 1 November 2026, so it is live and trading **[verified — communitypower.ie]**. Templederry Energy Resources is a member **[verified — search of Interreg and Tipperary press]**. SFF describes the related CRES entity as supplying "power locally" **[dated — SFF 2022]**.
- **What is absent:** any evidence that Templederry's own output reaches Templederry's own households.

## Distance to the river

**Possibly one contract, with a date on it.** The parts of a crossing are all in one county: a community generator, a community-owned licensed-scale supplier, and a fixed-term offtake that may end within about a year. When the Bord Gáis deal ends, selling Templederry's output to Community Power, and through it to Tipperary households, is a bilateral contract between two community bodies that already know each other. It is not a founding act. If that holds, it is the cleanest bridge in the survey. It depends on two unverified facts: the PPA's real end date, and whether Community Power can take a 4.6 MW wind offtake at all.
