# The Nantlle dossier — a valley caught mid-climb

_Published live (2026-07-04). The first of the two valley dossiers the second-cell treatment's verdict called for (condition: "a Nantlle dossier exists on the day the £1bn window opens"), built against the **GB Energy Local Power Plan window** specifically — the Welsh schemes' application rounds are closed (Ymestyn closed 6 February 2026; Ynni Cymru 2026–27 closed 21 May 2026, per the second seat's live check). **Process honesty:** this is a desk artefact, single-seat, no independent adversary has run on it. It is the desk half of the second-cell ladder's rung-0 output; the off-desk list at the end is the other half and is load-bearing. Every claim is tagged: **[verified]** = fetched live 2026-07-04 with source named; **[corpus]** = carried from the prior treatments' evidence; **[desk]** = inference or reading, unchecked in the world._

## The sharpest objection, on the face

**This dossier's own first finding is that the corpus was wrong about this valley — which is an argument against this dossier.** The second-cell fleet, run yesterday, graded Nantlle "civic rail, no energy — rung 0 of energy." Today's desk check found a community energy company mid-installation of a 311kW solar-and-battery scheme, funded £384,000 by the very grant programme the treatment discussed. If the corpus's biggest fact about the valley was stale within a day of publication, the presumption must be that this dossier is stale about something too, and the off-desk list is not an appendix — it is the dossier's claim to be trusted at all. A second edge on the same blade: the finding weakens the dossier's premise. The window targets "underserved areas or where there has historically been low take-up" **[verified — Local Power Plan, gov.uk]**, and a valley whose energy company just landed £384k of Welsh capital grant is spending its underserved status as this is written. The ask below leans on completion, not entry, for exactly that reason.

## The valley as found

**The correction first, because it re-rungs everything.** **Ynni Lleu CBC** — a community interest company limited by guarantee, incorporated 2 April 2019, registered at Yr Orsaf / Siop Griffiths, Water Street, Penygroes, status active **[verified — Companies House 11920947]** — was re-established in autumn 2025 with ten new members including seven new directors "to take over and lead the group" **[verified — GwyrddNi, posted 15/10/2025]**, and is installing **311kW of solar plus a 307kWh battery** on the roof of the old paper factory at the Penygroes industrial estate: expected output 311,000 kWh/yr, over 60% of the on-site tenants' annual electricity (tenants: Adra, Tŷ Gwyrddfai, Travis Perkins, Pero), estimated income ~£25,000/yr for community reinvestment. Funded by **Ynni Cymru (£384,000, 2024–25 round)** with support from DEG and Cyd Ynni **[verified — GwyrddNi; gov.wales grants-awarded list]**. Note the address: the valley's energy vehicle and its civic vehicle are the same building. **Correction owed to `NOTES-second-cell.md`, logged for the register:** the fleet's "Nantlle: civic rail, no energy" was a fourth fleet error at the second seat's standard — the valley is a live rung-4 instance, not a rung-0 blank.

**The civic rail, as the corpus had it, confirmed and extended:**

- **Yr Orsaf / Siop Griffiths Cyf** — a Community Benefit Society in Penygroes; the 1828 building bought with £53k community money in 2016; £900k raised since to renovate three buildings — café, accommodation, digital centre, repair café/make space **[verified — yrorsaf.cymru]**.
- **Community transport** — three EVs (minibus, 7-seater, 4-seater) with a paid Community Transport Officer, coordinated through the Dyffryn Nantlle Community Transport Partnership, Welsh Government and EU funded **[verified — yrorsaf.cymru project pages]**. The second-commodity treatment's pre-adaptation, standing.
- **Antur Nantlle Cyf** — the valley's development trust since 1991: business units, workshops, offices and shops across the valley; the former Barclays reopened 2018 as shop, deli and economic hub **[verified — anturnantlle.com]**. A second civic estate the corpus never mentioned — and a roof census candidate list in itself.
- **GwyrddNi** — the climate-assembly programme (DEG-delivered, Lottery-funded) with Yr Orsaf as the Dyffryn Nantlle delivery partner; the community's action plan prioritised community energy, and the **clean-energy mapping study is still "planned," not underway** **[verified — gwyrddni.cymru]**. The rung-0 valley dossier, in other words, has not been produced; this document is its desk sketch, not its substitute.
- **Tŷ Gwyrddfai** — Adra's decarbonisation hub on the same Penygroes estate; Cyd Ynni is in live discussion to add **250–500kW of solar** on its roof **[verified — Community Energy England, Sept 2024]**.
- **Upstream, the observed rung 5:** £20,000 of existing hydro-scheme profits allocated to advance a **100kW hydro on the Cwm Du river near Rhyd Ddu** (with a 35kW sibling in Blaenau) — preparatory archaeology and ecology work **[verified — Community Energy England, Sept 2024]**. Rhyd Ddu sits at the valley's head **[desk — geography read from a map, catchment not confirmed]**. Surplus seeding, the ladder's thinnest-evidenced rung, has a second live instance — and this one is off the Aber club's substrate, unlike Llanfairfechan.
- **What is still absent:** an Energy Local club. No club covers Penygroes, Talysarn or the Nantlle valley — checked against the full clubs list **[verified — energylocal.org.uk/clubs]**. The nearest are Gwyrfai (Bro Gwyrfai and Caeathro) over the ridge and Pendraw'r Byd on Llŷn.
- **The void, pre-demoted:** **Dorothea** — the flooded pit at Talysarn, privately held (Penygroes Holdings the last-reported owner), with development schemes announced and stalled serially since 1993 **[verified — North Wales Live archive; no post-2020 activity found]**. Enclosed-and-stalled, Glyn Rhonwy's shape at smaller scale. Per the house pattern, it bears nothing in this dossier.

## The ask, when the window opens

The window: up to **£1bn** through the GB Energy Local Power Plan — development grants (feasibility/early-stage), construction loans, partnership grants for community + local-government projects, and stake-buying support; grant schemes slated from spring 2026, the full **GBE Capital Toolkit in autumn 2026**; targeting "increasingly focused on underserved areas or those who are new to the sector" **[verified — gov.uk Local Power Plan]**. One route is open **today**: the **Expression of Interest form on gbe.gov.uk** — optional, non-committing, running through the first half of 2026 **[verified — gbe.gov.uk/express-interest]**. And one caveat the dossier must print rather than smooth: the existing GB Energy Community Fund is **England-only, delivered through regional Net Zero Hubs** **[verified — Community Energy England]** — the Welsh delivery route for the £1bn is not yet built, and it will be built during the Welsh merger. The GB-level window is the one live window *not* exposed to the counterparty rebuild; its Welsh front door, however, is.

1. **Now, costing nothing:** Ynni Lleu and Yr Orsaf each file a GBE EOI. This is the doctrine ("have the dossier ready the day it opens") in its cheapest executable form.
2. **The development-grant ask, drafted:** fund the **valley dossier's off-desk half** — the roof census across the two civic estates (Yr Orsaf's three buildings, Antur Nantlle's units, the chapels, Tŷ Gwyrddfai), the club-offer pricing around Ynni Lleu's export, and Cwm Du's remaining pre-consent work. Small money, all of it evidence-shaped, none of it construction risk.
3. **The partnership-grant fit:** the Tŷ Gwyrddfai 250–500kW discussion is already a community + housing-association + (plausibly) Cyngor Gwynedd configuration — the Local Power Plan's partnership category describes it almost verbatim. Converting that discussion into a signed application is also the merger doctrine (programmes into contracts before the churn) followed in the world.
4. **Honesty in the application itself:** Nantlle's claim on the *underserved* clause is "new to the sector," not "empty map" — one grant-funded CIC does not make a served valley, but the application should argue take-up history, not absence, because absence is now false.

## The off-desk list — what must be gathered on the ground

In priority order; items 1–3 gate the ask, the rest thicken it.

1. **Ynni Lleu's board, three questions:** commissioning date; the export arrangement (is there a metered export MPAN at all, or is the scheme wholly behind-the-meter?); and **has anyone priced an Energy Local club to them** — if declined, record the reason verbatim (this is the treatment's condition-1 discipline applied to a second board).
2. **The exportable-surplus number.** >60% self-consumption leaves at most ~120 MWh/yr for a match pool **[desk arithmetic]** — enough for a small club or not enough for any; nobody knows until the half-hourly profile exists.
3. **The mapping study's real status and ToR** — from Siôn at Yr Orsaf (the GwyrddNi facilitator). If the ToR is not yet written, put the rung-7 commons-share clause in it now.
4. **Cwm Du:** are the archaeology/ecology reports done; who owns the land; what did the £20k buy.
5. **Cyd Ynni membership:** is Ynni Lleu a member; does the consortium's surplus pool carry a Nantlle line.
6. **Grid:** SP Manweb headroom at the Penygroes primary; ANM/curtailment terms already accepted for the battery scheme; what a 100kW hydro addition would face.
7. **Dorothea's current owner and planning status** — the last verified activity is old; the pre-demotion should rest on a current fact.
8. **Household tier:** EPC clusters and fuel-poverty mapping for Penygroes/Talysarn/Llanllyfni, so the conceded-cohort line is priced, not gestured at.
