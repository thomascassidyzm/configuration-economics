# The whole-effect check: a service an AI agent can offer any initiative

_Forming (2026-11-21). A starting note for a six-hat run of the world computer. It follows [run-003](/world-computer?run=run-003), which worked only the first of seven draft steps for AI agents and left four unworked: tracing where hidden costs land, checking the whole effect of an initiative, comparing ownership arrangements, and proposing the small move that can be undone. This note folds those four into one service and asks the room to make it concrete. Run-004 checked some of it and gave its sources; every other factual claim stays unverified until a run checks it. The current design is under "The service, as the run left it"; the first draft is kept above it for the record._

_Moved on 2026-11-21 by a [six-hat run](/world-computer?run=run-004): the agent no longer judges an initiative; it tags and questions the initiative's own draft under a stated stopping sentence, and the page itself can be withdrawn._

---

## Where this starts

Tom Cassidy's framing, given at a conference in Bologna on 21 November 2026, put in this note's words except where quoted:

- **The measure is possibility.** Some arrangements of the same matter open more possibilities than others. The atoms in a phone were all in the ground; they were dug up and assembled, and the assembled phone lets its holder do things the ore never could. (The idea of counting how much assembling a thing took comes from the chemist Lee Cronin's Assembly Theory.) So the question to ask of any move is which way it pushes the set of futures that people and living systems can still reach.
- **The aim, in his words:** "More possibilities for all and less to none".
- **The DNA economy.** A circular economy keeps materials going round at a steady level. Tom's extension is a loop that gains each time round, the way living things reuse the same atoms while what they can do keeps growing: each turn of the loop leaves behind know-how, tools or trust that makes the next turn open more. What grows is the pattern, not the amount of material or energy used.
- **Forward-facing and blame-agnostic.** The present state is the starting point. History is information about where everyone now stands, never a debt.
- **Ownership is an arrangement.** Who owns a thing is one option among several that can be compared by the same question.
- **It plays nicely with every other initiative.** Nobody has to be wrong. The check below is offered as a service to people already doing the work.

## The service, as first drafted

An initiative (a community energy scheme, a repair and reuse network, a wellbeing budget, a land trust, a company's product line) asks: *what is the whole effect of what we are doing?* An AI agent, working only from public information and what the initiative itself hands over, contacting nobody else, returns one page. The page has five parts.

1. **What it opens, for whom, how soon.** The possibilities the initiative creates, and for which people and living systems.
2. **Where the costs that are not in the price land.** Each cost the initiative does not pay itself, followed to whoever carries it: neighbours, non-members, another place, a later year, a river, a soil.
3. **Indirect effects.** What changes one or two steps away: what people stop doing, what the saved money gets spent on, who loses custom, what gets easier or harder for the next initiative.
4. **The arrangement.** Who owns and decides now, set beside two or three other arrangements that exist somewhere for the same kind of thing (private, public, commons, trust, shared), and which way each has pushed where it was tried.
5. **The small move that can be undone.** One change the initiative could try that would open more for those currently getting less, and how to reverse it if it fails. Plus: does each turn of the initiative make its next turn easier (does the loop gain), and is anything at risk that could never be rebuilt?

The page gives no score and no verdict on the initiative. It ends in questions the initiative can answer better than the agent can.

## The service, as the run left it

The run kept the five questions but changed who answers them. In the first draft the agent writes claims about the initiative. The run found that this makes the page read as a verdict, lets weak figures look checked, and describes people who never spoke. In the revised design the initiative writes its own answers and the agent tags them and asks questions. The run also recommends dropping the name "whole-effect check", because no method traces a whole effect and a funder skimming the title may take the page as the full picture. A new name has not been chosen.

**The agent's steps, in order.** Two agents given the same initiative should follow the same steps.

1. **Send a four-line card**, saying what the initiative gets and what the agent needs, with one-tap answers: yes, no, or "show me an example first". The example is a finished page for a different kind of initiative, which could be built from a public sector report such as Community Energy England's annual [State of the Sector report](https://communityenergyengland.org/publications/state-of-the-sector-2025/). That report has been produced since 2017, and the latest is dated 17 September 2025 ([series page](https://communityenergyengland.org/state-of-the-sector/)). A "no" is recorded as "declined" and no page is made.
2. **Record who asked and in what role.** The agent cannot verify this because it contacts nobody, so the page carries a visible line: "Requested by [role], not confirmed as speaking for the initiative." The requester chooses one of three outputs: a shareable page, a private cover note, or "not enough to go on". A "not enough to go on" reply must say what the agent would need, so that it is a next step and not a bare refusal.
3. **List the sources before tracing anything.** This means public documents and records handed over, each marked as the initiative's own, public and independent, or neither, with its date. For repair networks, for example, Repair Café International publishes annual reports, a starter kit and a map of groups ([annual reports](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/category/annual-report/)). The Restart Project publishes annual reports, repair data and open reference data on the impact of repairs ([FAQ and data](https://therestartproject.org/faq/)). No equivalent list was confirmed for wellbeing budgets.
4. **Write one sentence saying where tracing stops and why.** This replaces the first draft's "one or two steps away", which has no source. Established methods set no fixed number of steps. ISO 14044 for life-cycle assessment requires a study to state its cut-off criteria and test how they affect the result ([ISO 14044:2006](https://archive.org/stream/gov.in.is.iso.14044.2006/is.iso.14044.2006_djvu.txt)). The social return on investment guide and the EU Better Regulation Toolbox, chapter 8, rely on materiality and proportionality, and the SROI guide also allows for effects fading over time ([SROI guide, 2009](https://ifcsia.org/wp-content/uploads/pdf/publications/Measuring-Value-A-Guide-to-Social-Return-on-Investment.pdf); [EU Toolbox ch. 8, 2023](https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/BRT-2023-Chapter%208-Methodologies%20for%20analysing%20impacts%20in%20IAs%20evaluations%20and%20fitness%20checks_0.pdf)). Supply-chain methods that cut off at a chosen number of tiers were not checked.
5. **Have the initiative draft its own boxes**, answering the five questions in its own words.
6. **Tag each claim** in the draft as "your records", "public and independent", or "our estimate", quoting any public source in full. Each tag limits what the page may say:
   - A "your records" figure may appear only beside the initiative's own stated assumption.
   - A "public and independent" cost may be stated at the level of a role, with the source quoted.
   - An "our estimate" may appear only as a question or a range.

   For example, Repair Café International's figure of about 420,000 kg of waste prevented in 2019 comes from 2,000 cafés × 12 meetings × 25 objects × 70% success × an assumed 1 kg per object, while its own 2019 repair log showed 63% success ([news item](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/repair-cafes-prevent-420000-kilos-of-waste-in-2019/); [2019 summary report](https://www.repaircafe.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Annual_report_2019_Summary_ENGLISH_12_2020-3.pdf)). The page would set that figure beside its assumptions. The agent's disagreements with the draft go in a private note to the requester, not on the page.
7. **List as questions** the effects that public sources mention and the draft omits. This becomes the sixth box, "Not in your draft".
8. **Name the nearest ownership comparison and one small trial**, saying how the trial would be undone and who must agree to it. Evidence on ownership is thin. It includes a review of 91 commons studies (Cox et al., 2010), comparisons of forest commons (Chhatre and Agrawal) and reviews of water privatisation (for example Araral) ([Cox et al.](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/992c/afca81f665d9279e48f741611b78c6eb3110.pdf); [review](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/su9071287)). It is specific to each resource and says little about trusts and cooperatives. No single source compares all five arrangements across resources. The page should therefore say where the evidence runs out rather than draw a lesson from it. For some initiatives, such as a publicly held wellbeing budget, the comparison may not fit at all, and the page should say so.

**The page, laid out for a phone:**

- a requested-by line
- a valid-until date, twelve months on, with "withdrawable by the requester at any time"
- the stopping sentence
- six boxes: the initiative's five, tagged, plus "Not in your draft"
- open questions
- a method block, holding the source list, the stopping sentence and the tag rules
- a correction slot for anyone the page describes

People appear by role only. A withdrawn page keeps its address and shows only "Withdrawn by requester on [date]". A page made for someone outside the initiative expires after a year unless the initiative adopts it.

**Yearly reruns.** The method block is reused word for word each year. Any change to it is raised as an open question that the initiative can accept or decline. This way the trend compares the initiative with itself, not one agent's choices with another's. The run proposed one firmer answer to "does the loop gain": count the open questions closed with a source each year against the new ones opened. This is untested. No method was found for "could anything never be rebuilt", so the page asks it only as a question.

## Why an initiative might want it

Published reports rarely state where their tracing stopped. The run found no community energy or repair report on social return that states its cut-off rule in one plain sentence. The nearest was the generic NEF guide, and a Repair Café Wales report could not be opened in full ([NEF guide](https://new-economicsf.files.svdcdn.com/production/files/aff3779953c5b88d53_cpm6v3v71.pdf); [Repair Café Wales](https://cydinnovation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Repair-Cafe-FINAL-06-26.pdf)). New Zealand's Wellbeing Budget 2019 names the gaps in its dashboard, and Treasury's CBAx guidance asks for unmonetised impacts to be reported. However, no wellbeing-budget publication was found that says which indirect effects were traced and where tracing stopped ([Wellbeing Budget 2019](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/publications/wellbeing-budget/wellbeing-budget-2019); [CBAx guide](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2018-09/cbax-guide-sep18.pdf)).

The page could offer an initiative four things:

- a stopping sentence it can repeat to funders
- sturdier figures, such as a measured weight per object from open repair data in place of an assumed one kilogram
- an honest "what could not be found" line
- a trend series from cheap yearly reruns

A shared method block would also let funders compare how far different kinds of initiative traced, not how well each told its story. None of this has been tried.

## Risks the design must carry

These are risks to the design, not charges against anyone.

- **Read as a verdict.** A page with no score may still be read as a verdict. Gaps in the record land hardest on small, thinly documented bodies, such as a repair group with no annual report.
- **Refusing those who most need it.** A minimum number of sources would either turn away the bodies that most need an outside view or become a reassuring count with no benchmark behind it.
- **Selective records.** The initiative controls what it hands over, so incomplete logs or only the good months can make a page look balanced.
- **Uncheckable sources.** Public sources may be old, partial or written by rivals, and the agent may invent a plausible source. Quoting sources in full moves the checking to a time-poor reader on a phone.
- **Leaning either way.** The blame-agnostic tone may push the agent to soften a cost that documents state plainly. Fear of flattering may push it to overstate a cost it can only guess at.
- **People who never spoke.** Because the agent contacts nobody, those who carry costs are described from documents only. The SROI guide treats talking to those affected as what gives such work credibility. No consent rule can reach rivers, soils or future residents.
- **Caveats lost in forwarding.** A busy volunteer may approve and forward a draft, and the caveats get lost on the way.
- **Uninvited pages.** A rival or sceptical neighbour may commission a page the initiative never asked for. Because pages cost almost nothing, someone could also make many and publish the most flattering.
- **Trials that are hard to undo.** A trial called reversible may be hard to withdraw once households have budgeted around it, for example reserved capacity for fuel-poor households. A funder may also treat "can be undone" as a reason to stop early.
- **Unanswered questions.** Questions may go unanswered by an unstaffed body, leaving the page as the only account.

## What run-004 was asked to settle

- The exact steps the agent takes for each part, in order, so that a different agent given the same initiative would produce much the same page. *(Steps drafted above. Whether two agents match is untested.)*
- What the agent must be handed, and what it can find for itself. *(Partly settled by steps 3 and 5.)*
- How "one or two steps away" is bounded, so that the tracing stops somewhere. *(Replaced by the stopping sentence in step 4.)*
- What stops the page doing harm: to the initiative, to people named in it, to those left out of it. *(Partly settled by role-only naming, private disagreements, withdrawal, expiry and the correction slot. Those left out remain open.)*
- Whether this is worth more to an initiative than what it already does for itself. *(Untested.)*

## What is not known

- Whether indirect effects can be traced reliably from public information at all.
- Whether a page with no score is usable by anyone who has to make a decision.
- Whether the "does the loop gain" question can be answered with anything firmer than opinion. Counting questions closed against questions opened is one untested candidate.
- Whether two agents following the same steps produce the same page.
- What a single community energy scheme or repair group publishes, and what public documents exist for wellbeing budgets.
- How to give rivers, soils and future residents a say on a page that contacts nobody.
- Whether the Bologna and Lee Cronin attributions are accurate. The run did not check them.
- Nothing in the procedure has yet been tried by hand.

## Next check

Two agents run the eight steps separately on the same three initiatives: one community energy scheme, one repair group and one wellbeing budget. They then compare their pages line by line, noting where the stopping sentence, the tags and the "Not in your draft" questions differ.
