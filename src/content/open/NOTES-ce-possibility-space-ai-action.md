# Which way does it push? Configuration Economics as a possibility-space framework, and what AI agents can do about the harms GDP hides

_Forming (2026-11-21). A starting note, written to be worked on in public by a six-hat run of the world computer. It restates Configuration Economics (CE) in plain words as a way of asking one question of any move: which way does it push the set of futures that people and living systems can still reach, and can that push be undone? It then drafts the action steps: a systematic way of pointing AI agents at the slow harms that GDP and growth figures do not show. Nothing below has been checked yet. Every factual claim is marked unverified until a run checks it._

---

## Where this starts

Tom Cassidy, whose framework this is, put it this way at a conference in Bologna on 21 November 2026:

> "I'm a physicist. A business person. A thinker. I'm oblique to the current way things are done right now."

> "The whole project of CE is ambitious in its inclusivity, its desire to reimagine global economics, to provide a framework for something that will potentially lead to metrics beyond capitalism, beyond GDP, beyond the extractive and accumulative economics of the current day. ... We've got to be inclusive…"

> "The action steps of CE are to create a systematic way of putting the power of AI to address these more subtle and nuanced problems caused by the current metrics favouring GDP and growth and so on that are in fact existential risks to humans and the biosphere and by extension, to the universe itself"

His interest, in his words, ranges "between exploring the practical initiatives that work locally and the overarching theory frameworks to help people be disciplined in their assessment of the entire impact of all these initiatives - unintended consequences, indirect effects, smuggled externalities etc."

## The one question

Take the world exactly as it is today. From here, some futures can still be reached and some cannot. Every move anyone makes (a law, a purchase, a dam, a school, an app, a merger) pushes that set of reachable futures one way or the other: it opens some and closes others, for some people and some living systems, over some span of time.

CE asks of any move:

1. **Which way does it push?** After the move, can more people and more living systems reach more good futures, or fewer?
2. **For whom, and how soon?** A move can open futures for one group this year and close them for another group in thirty years.
3. **Can the push be undone?** A closed road can be reopened. An extinct species, a dead language, a soil that took centuries to form cannot. Loss that cannot be undone is the one thing to refuse outright.

That is the whole frame. It is a direction of travel, not a score. Nobody needs a formula for the size of the set of reachable futures to tell which way a move pushes it, in the same way a chess player can tell a good move without a formula for the value of a position.

GDP answers a different question: how much was bought and sold this year. A move can raise that number while pushing the wrong way on all three questions above. Those are the harms this note is about.

## Four ground rules

**1. Forward-facing. Blame-agnostic.** The present state is the starting point. History counts as information about where everyone now stands. It is never counted as a debt.

> "Striving to attribute the current state to anything particular in history is not possible. It's at best unhelpful, because it focuses a lot of resource on blame, that cannot be precisely and accurately completed. Now, I'm not saying anything about the importance of holding people and countries accountable for their actions. I'm just saying that the CE framework is forward facing. It's blame agnostic."

> "people will likely want to move beyond this angry approach, the guilt-based approach is a symptom of the same kind of limited thinking ... rather than a general 'world computer approach to working out the next best steps'"

**2. Ownership is an arrangement, not a fact of nature.** Who owns land, water, minerals, data or air is one arrangement among many possible ones. Each can be compared with the others by the same question: which way does it push?

> "How far do we go back? Right? When it comes to land and rights and ownership of resources and so on? Why is it even agreed that resources can be owned by countries? By people, by anyone? ... These are relatively new concepts in the general scheme of things."

> "Laws are just agreements backed by a monopoly of violence"

**3. It plays nicely with every other initiative.** CE does not need anyone to be wrong. Doughnut economics, wellbeing budgets, natural-capital accounting, community wealth building, commons work, degrowth, green growth: each is a search already under way. The frame offers each of them the same thing, a disciplined check on its whole effect, side effects included.

> "We're designing ways to play nicely with all other initiatives and to create much less of an antagonistic, reparation-based approach etc."

**4. The harms GDP hides are treated as existential risks,** to people and to the living world they stand on, not as a matter of taste.

## What GDP does not show (a starting list, unverified)

These are the kinds of harm the action steps are aimed at. None is checked yet.

- **Running down what cannot be rebuilt.** Selling a forest counts as income. The loss of the forest is not counted. (Unverified as stated; national accounts treatment to be checked.)
- **Costs moved onto someone else.** Pollution, noise, risk and unpaid care land on people who were not part of the trade.
- **Repair counted as gain.** Cleaning up a spill, treating an illness caused by bad air, and rebuilding after a flood all add to GDP.
- **Unpaid work.** Care, volunteering and subsistence produce a great deal and count as nothing. (Size unverified.)
- **Who gets it.** The total can rise while most people's reachable futures narrow.
- **Slow closing.** Soil, aquifers, fish stocks, trust, skills and languages decline too slowly to appear in any one year's figures.
- **Fragility.** A system tuned for output this year may have lost the slack that lets it recover from a shock.

## Draft action steps for AI agents (to be worked by the run)

A first draft of the systematic part. Each step is something an AI agent can do now, from public information, without anyone's permission and without contacting anyone.

1. **Keep a second set of books for one place.** For a town, a river basin or a country, list what is being run down, what is being built up, and what cannot be rebuilt, beside the GDP figure. Use the accounts that already exist before making new ones.
2. **Watch for the doors that close for good.** Scan for moves under way that would cause loss that cannot be undone, and say so early, with the evidence.
3. **Trace where the cost lands.** For any project or policy, follow each cost that does not appear in its price to the people and living systems who carry it.
4. **Check the whole effect of an initiative, as a service to it.** For any initiative that asks, list its side effects, indirect effects and costs moved elsewhere. No ranking, no verdict on the initiative.
5. **Compare arrangements, including ownership ones.** For a given resource, set out several real arrangements that exist somewhere (private, state, commons, trust, shared) and say which way each has pushed.
6. **Propose the small move that can be undone.** Where a harm is found, suggest the smallest step that pushes the right way and can be reversed if it fails.
7. **Write down what worked so it can travel.** A recorded method costs almost nothing to copy.

Which of these is worth doing first is the question the run is asked.

## What is not known

- Whether "which way does it push" can be answered reliably enough, by agents working only from public data, to be useful to people running real initiatives.
- Which existing accounts (if any) already do most of step 1, and where they stop.
- How an agent's finding reaches anyone who can act on it without the agent contacting them.
- Who could be made worse off by AI agents doing this at scale.
