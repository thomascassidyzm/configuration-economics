# The Corris dossier — membership, not founding

_Published live (2026-07-04). The second of the two valley dossiers the second-cell treatment's verdict called for — and the one the verdict ordered **re-argued at the right tier first**: the independent adversary caught the fleet placing the Machynlleth club "five miles south" of a village its own coverage line includes ("Machynlleth, Pennal, Corris, Eglwysfach & surrounds"). So this is not the dossier of an honest empty. It is the dossier of a village already inside a club's catchment, in a valley that has held its own regional organ for longer than Gwynedd has — and its job is to say what **membership-not-founding** looks like, and to stop the £1bn window's "underserved" clause from misfiring here. Built against the **GB Energy Local Power Plan window**; the Welsh rounds are closed (Ymestyn, 6 February 2026; Ynni Cymru 2026–27, 21 May 2026). **Process honesty:** desk artefact, single-seat, no independent adversary. Tags as in the Nantlle dossier: **[verified]** fetched live 2026-07-04, **[corpus]**, **[desk]**._

## The sharpest objection, on the face

**"Corris is covered" is a coverage line on a website, and this dossier is built on it — the same class of evidence the fleet got burned by yesterday.** The adversary's correction rests on the club's published coverage area, not on a membership roll: the desk cannot see whether a single Corris household actually belongs to the club. Coverage and take-up are different facts — the whole £1bn targeting clause exists because they differ — and if the roll shows zero or near-zero Corris members, then at the tier that matters Corris is functionally unserved after all, the correction inverts back, and this dossier's central move ("join, don't found") changes from a completed argument into an unexplained refusal — a village-scale sibling of the Llanberis nine-year question. Off-desk item 1 is therefore not colour; it decides which dossier this is.

## The valley as found

Corris (population ~600 **[corpus, desk-derived then; unrevisited]**) is a slate village in the Dulas valley, administratively in Gwynedd, functionally in the Dyfi. What the desk finds is not an empty map but a **complete Dyfi organ stack, older than Gwynedd's**:

- **The club, live and recruiting.** Energy Local Machynlleth covers "Machynlleth, Pennal, Corris, Eglwysfach & surrounds"; generator **Esgairweddan, a 60kW farm hydro in Pennal**; members pay 8.5–15.5p/kWh for matched local power with 100Green topping up; free smart meter on joining; claimed savings 10–30%; the club page is actively soliciting households — "no obligation" register-interest, contact Andy Rowland **[verified — energylocal.org.uk/machynlleth + key-info]**. Not closed, not waitlisted: the door is open.
- **The organ.** **Ecodyfi** — locally-controlled regeneration body, est. 1998 out of a CAT / Dulas Ltd / Powys partnership, mission "sustainable community regeneration in the Dyfi valley" — is the body that established the club **[verified — ecodyfi.wales]**. Founded 1998: the Dyfi's continuity organ predates DEG by nearly two decades. For the treatment's condition 4 (count the catchment), this is a second in-Wales observation of the organ shape, independently precipitated.
- **The asset-owning mutual.** **Bro Dyfi Community Renewables** — a registered community benefit society (FCA 29283R) holding two wind turbines (75kW Vestas, 2003, above CAT; 500kW Nordtank, 2008, Mynydd Glandulas) plus a 300kW solar array (2023) — ~875kW total. Its **Community Energy Fund's eligible area names Corris first**: "the Community Council areas of Corris, Glantwymyn, Machynlleth and Cadfarch" — £1,000 grants to 11 local organisations in 2025. **A future share offer is planned** **[verified — bdcr.org.uk]**.
- **The neighbour.** CAT sits at Pantperthog, between Corris and Machynlleth, and took £224,230 from the Ynni Cymru 2024–25 round **[verified — gov.wales grants list]**.
- **Corris's own civic fabric** — thinner, real: the **Corris Institute** (the renovated village centre — meetings, computer room, classes, dinners); **Canolfan Corris Cyf** (the 1872 old school, a company since 1992, hostel and education use); the community council; the Craft Centre and the tourism-leased caverns up the valley **[verified/desk — directory-grade sources; no site visits]**.
- **The void, pre-demoted differently.** Braich Goch and the Corris workings are *enclosed-and-working* — leased to tourism operators (King Arthur's Labyrinth, mine tours) — not enclosed-and-stalled like Dorothea or Glyn Rhonwy **[desk]**. Either way the house pattern holds: the voids bear nothing here. Their one live relevance is rung 7: any *regional* slate-void characterisation must include Corris's workings, and Corris should be a beneficiary of that commons, never its solo commissioner.

## What membership-not-founding looks like

The founding acts are all in the past tense, done by others: the club is founded (Ecodyfi), the mutual is founded (BDCR, 2003), the organ is founded (1998), the benefit fund already names Corris. So the ladder here reads as a **consumption schedule, not a construction project** — and every line on it is cheaper than the cheapest founding act:

1. **A household joins the club.** A register-interest form, a supplier switch to 100Green, a free smart meter. No constitution, no share offer, no board, no meeting. This is the second-cell ladder's rung 2 with the founding stripped out — and unlike Llanberis's version it is *observed open* today.
2. **A household becomes an owner.** BDCR's planned share offer is the priced path from member to part-owner of the valley's generation — joining an existing society's raise, not running one.
3. **A civic body consumes services.** The Institute or Canolfan Corris applies to the BDCR fund (the rail already reaches them: 11 grants went out in 2025) and, if a roof case exists, hosts a free-roof-anchor install owned by BDCR or the club's generator pool — one decision-maker, zero capex, the Egni shape.
4. **The village never founds anything.** If off-desk work surfaces a Corris-side generation case (Institute roof, school roof, a Dulas micro-hydro), its natural owner is BDCR or Ecodyfi — vehicles with boards, accountants and FCA registrations already paid for. A new "Ynni Corris" would be a founding act the bridge test forbids when a standing mutual next door can hold the asset.

What Corris genuinely lacks is not access but **local generation feeding the match pool**: one 60kW hydro is a small pool, and a Corris membership drive without new generation dilutes everyone's match percentage **[desk — the club's member/generation ratio is off-desk item 2]**.

## The ask, when the window opens — mostly a refusal

The window: the same up-to-£1bn Local Power Plan machinery as the Nantlle dossier (development grants, partnership grants, loans, stake-buying; Capital Toolkit autumn 2026; EOI open now **[verified — gov.uk; gbe.gov.uk]**), with the same printed caveat: the existing GBE Community Fund runs **England-only through Net Zero Hubs**, and the Welsh delivery route will be built during the Welsh merger.

1. **The dossier's first deliverable is a sentence for the funder, not an application:** *Corris is not underserved at household tier — it is inside a live, open club — and a Corris-labelled grant claiming the underserved clause would be the anti-rails targeting wall operating as the treatment predicted: money routed by map-emptiness at the wrong tier.* Saying this out loud is the dossier following the treatment's own two-tier lens instead of gaming it.
2. **What is honestly askable, at the right tier and by the right owner:** a **generation-tier** ask, filed by Ecodyfi/BDCR for the Dyfi (not "for Corris") — new generation feeding the club's match pool, with the Corris civic roofs and any Dulas micro-hydro among the candidate sites, and **repowering the 2003 75kW Vestas** (23 years old) as the obvious stake-buying/loan-shaped item **[desk — repowering intent unconfirmed, off-desk item 4]**.
3. **Now, costing nothing:** Ecodyfi files the GBE EOI for the Dyfi; Corris households join the club without waiting for any window at all. The cheapest move in either valley dossier is a Corris standing order.
4. **What this dossier declines to invent:** a Corris energy vehicle, a Corris feasibility study, a Corris-labelled application. Each has a standing owner one valley over; inventing a local duplicate to fit a funding clause is supply-led grant-chasing — the failure mode the whole series exists to name.

## The off-desk list — what must be gathered on the ground

1. **The membership roll** (Andy Rowland, machynlleth@energylocal.org.uk): how many Corris households are club members. This decides the dossier — see the objection on the face. If ~zero: *why* — awareness, smart-meter signal, supplier stickiness, or refusal — recorded verbatim, per the house discipline.
2. **The club's headroom:** Esgairweddan's output against current membership; how many Corris joiners the match pool absorbs before dilution; whether the club wants a drive or dreads one.
3. **Ecodyfi's Corris pipeline:** anything already scoped in the village (roof audits, hydro looks, retrofit lists); and whether Ecodyfi will carry the Dyfi EOI.
4. **BDCR:** share-offer timing and terms; repowering intent for the Vestas; whether the fund's Corris take-up (which of the 11 grants, if any, landed in Corris) matches the eligibility line.
5. **The civic roofs:** Institute and Canolfan Corris — roof condition, daytime load, ownership cleanliness (free-roof-anchor pre-screens).
6. **Smart-meter and signal coverage in the village** — the known rural blocker for half-hourly clubs **[desk inference; needs a doorstep, not a database]**.
7. **Grid:** the Machynlleth-area constraint status on SP Manweb's map; export headroom for any new Dulas-side generation.
