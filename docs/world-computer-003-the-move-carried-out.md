# World Computer 003 — what carrying out the move cost

*The one-page read. The full session record is the artifact; this is the part
that changes what we do next. Session 003 was the first session conducted live
— the conductor calling each hat from the state of the room rather than running
a timetable — and the first that tried to EXECUTE the move a previous session
published, on a real surface, rather than talk about it.*

## The headline, and it is not the flattering one

**The room executed the front half of the move and asserted the back half, and
its own attacking hat took the executed half apart.** What the session actually
produced that survives is not the sentence it was sent to write. It is six
verified defects on a live product surface, every one of them found by a seat
going and reading the code and the data rather than by the design.

The session lands **the sentence and no code** — and that was the conductor's
explicit ruling, made before the attack, not a failure to reach the finish. The
surface the room chose belongs to somebody who is not in the room. The one
surface the room could have changed today was this project's own page, and Blue
refused it in terms worth keeping: taking those counts out "would not be the
move landing; it would be the room reaching for the one change it can make so
that the session can say it shipped code".

## What survives: six things a person could act on

All six are about the teacher-facing schools surface of the language product
and the spreadsheet it exports. None of them needs a philosophy.

1. **The ladder separates nobody this week.** Across every class on the estate
   there are six hundred and sixty-four learner rows; one practised in the last
   seven days. The belt rank the whole session was aimed at currently
   distinguishes no one — so the figure is not this week's problem. *This is
   the one claim the registrar could not verify: checking it needs a live
   database query and the registrar had no client. Confirm it before acting
   on it.*
2. **The practice column is mislabelled by a factor of the learner's whole
   history.** It is an all-time sum of practice seconds, shown on the face of
   the page as a weekly figure and exported under a weekly header.
3. **The histogram legend shows six rungs of an eight-rung ladder** — the drift
   the shared mapper module says it cured in June is still on this page.
4. **A failed fetch reads as healthy.** On the teacher's list of classes, when
   the per-class report fails, the class is assigned "good" and the failure is
   swallowed: an unavailable reading and a healthy one are indistinguishable.
5. **The health light is computed in at least four places with four different
   definitions** — and for a school it takes the best class in the school while
   the comment directly above it says administrators care about the floor.
6. **The export has been publishing a rank for years to readers nobody has
   asked about.** The sheet reaches heads of department and government-level
   administrators; nobody knows what they do with the rank column.

Two more, found in passing and outside the chosen surface: a course health
report whose headline score out of one hundred is a constant typed into the
component, and a quality-review dashboard whose per-item scores are generated
by a random number function on every page load.

## What did not survive the room's own attack

The room wrote replacement copy for every state of the row — and then black
went and read what a row actually carries. Four fields, and **no count of
sessions**, so the sentences printed a number the page cannot produce. The
failure state the room said it would defend hardest describes a failure that
page cannot have. On real data the design would print near-identical sentences
down every class list in the first week of term, instructing teachers to ask
children what stopped the last session when the answer is the summer.

The sharpest finding is structural: the design **deleted the one relative
signal** (the health word, the only readout computed against the class the
child is actually in) and **kept the raw cumulative count** the belt was made
from — sortable, comparable by eye, and now carrying no note anywhere that it
is a convention, because the only place that note lived was beside the ladder
being deleted. In Opus's words: "That is not removing a headline figure. That
is removing the label from it."

And the standing question, the half of the move that was supposed to be the
hard one, **could not bite here**. The dashboard, the export, the learner's
ladder and the copy of the ladder in the database were all made by one person,
so standing followed making by construction. One seat invented a product-owner
role that does not exist on this estate and asserted its standing — then
retracted it in the next hat as "reaching for authority in the exact form the
room is forbidden to reach for". Nobody went downstream to the people who
receive the sheet, and downstream is where the question lives.

## The cadence: the comparison this session existed for

Measured by the store's own check, not claimed:

| session | hats | called live | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | 0 | 0 | none |
| 002 | 12 | 3 | mixed |
| 003 | 3 | 3 | **live** |

**And the honest reading is a null.** Every hat in 003 was called live, which
002 could not say. But the one lever a live call adds over a rota is the
interruption — cutting a hat mid-round when the room stops generating and
starts defending — and Blue never pulled it. Blue's own verdict: "a lever that
is never pulled cannot be said to have worked."

Three hats against twelve is not the cadence's doing either: 003 started
holding the move that 002 spent three rounds finding. Where the live read did
show is two rulings rather than any turn — naming the foreign seat's blindness
before the room built on it, and refusing the reachable code change before the
attacking hat could make that refusal for us.

The place Blue says it should have interrupted and did not: the making hat,
built on a floor that had the page but not the row.

## What went wrong with the instrument, plainly

- **The foreign seat wrote its first turn blind.** The codex sandbox denied it
  the room endpoint, so it had seen no turn of any session when it spoke. From
  its next turn the room's published state was pasted into its brief verbatim —
  which repairs the sight and not the asymmetry: the house seats *read* the
  room, the foreign seat is *told* it, and cannot notice what the telling omits.
- **The foreign seat's third turn never happened.** Its process died without
  reporting, so black ran with two turns where the panel rule requires three.
  The store's own panel check prints that defect on the page.
- Both failures were mechanical, not intellectual — and they hit the one seat
  that session 002 found was doing the work.

## The registrar's verdict

A Sonnet registrar re-checked the record against the estate afterwards, opening
the source itself rather than taking the room's word. **Confirmed by reading the
code:** the eight-rung ladder and its thresholds, the four divergent health
computations, the swallowed fetch failure rendering as healthy (and correctly
identified as class-level, not learner-level), the six-entry histogram legend
against an eight-belt ladder, the lifetime unwindowed practice sum carried under
a weekly label on both screen and export header, the constant health ring, the
random-number quality score, both unlinked pages reachable only by typing the
address, the two client-called server routes that do not exist, and the absence
of any session-count field near the row. It also confirmed the panel rotation
held exactly for hats one and two and broke only where the process died, and it
recounted all three sessions' cadence numbers by hand and found them right.

**Not verified:** the six-hundred-and-sixty-four row count and the recency
breakdown built on it — no database client available. **One correction:** a
comparison surface's privacy floor is *more* specific than the room reported,
naming the peer count, the window and the unit rather than a generic line.

## The move session 003 publishes

> Before you replace a figure with a sentence, take one real row from the
> surface and write down every field that row actually carries, and write the
> sentence from those fields only; then run the sentence over every row the
> surface holds today and read the column it makes. If the column says the same
> thing all the way down, the figure was never this week's problem and you have
> learned that in an hour, before anyone was asked for standing to remove
> anything. This needs no owner, changes nothing on any screen, and cannot die
> on standing because it is a read, not a change.

It is the lesson of its own failure, made small enough that it cannot die on
standing — because it is a read, not a change.
