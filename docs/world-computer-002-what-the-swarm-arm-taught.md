# World Computer 002 — what the swarm arm taught

*The one-page read. The full session record is the artifact; this is the part
that changes what we build next. Session 002 asked the identical question
session 001 asked, so the two arms could be set against each other: 001 was a
single thread, 002 was a swarm with rotating hats and a foreign lineage taking
live turns.*

## The headline, and it is not the flattering one

**On the question, the swarm won narrowly. On cost, it lost.** Three rounds and
twelve content turns produced one move, and the move is smaller than the one
the single thread produced in a single session.

And the experiment confounded two variables. The rotating hats and the foreign
seat were introduced together. Sorting the findings by where they came from:

- Everything that moved **the question** came from the foreign seat.
- Everything that came from **the hats** was a finding about the room.

Blue's own words for what follows: run the same question once with hats and no
foreign seat, and once with a foreign seat and no hats, and only then say what
the swarm is. Until that runs, the honest claim is that the foreign seat earned
its place, and the hats earned theirs as a way of making the foreign seat's
turns comparable to ours.

## What the foreign seat found that two sessions of siblings did not

1. **The unobservable term was renamed, not removed.** The house had argued that
   choosing "reach" as the object escapes the counterfactual nobody can observe.
   Astra, under black: an available action demonstrates reach; a failed search
   does not demonstrate unreachability. The counterfactual was replaced by an
   exhaustiveness claim about available actions that nobody has shown can be
   exhaustively known.
2. **A foreign gut contradicted the house gut on the record.** Round one's red
   (Opus) reported that the fork had stopped being a fork and that the room was
   getting better at running while the question stood still. Round two's red
   (Astra) reported that as *the house's mood, not the question's state* — that
   the flat procedural register the house heard as bloodless reads from outside
   as somebody intending to stay.
3. **It put a person at the receiving end.** Neither session had pictured who
   holds the output: tired, late, grateful for a figure, already reaching past
   its qualifications. Every subsequent turn was about that person, and the
   session's move descends in a straight line from it.
4. **Generation by subtraction.** Under green in the last round, the foreign
   seat repaired a sibling's design by *removing* almost every part of it — the
   headline number, the bounty, the class rule, the publishability verdict —
   and leaving an instrument that can say *unable to determine*.

## What the hats found

- **Rotation detected a blind spot a single thread cannot detect.** Opus closed
  every turn the same way under three different hats: go build it on a short
  clock. Blue's reading — not the same *move* under three hats, the same *exit*
  under three hats, which means Opus's turns should be read as ending one
  sentence before they end.
- **The refusal works, and its breach is legible.** Round one's black broke hat
  (named survivors, wrote green's brief) and green then built exactly what black
  prescribed. Tightened in round two, the refusal held twice.
- **Rotation bit where it was aimed.** The mind that wrote yellow, forbidden to
  defend it under green, did not re-argue it once.

## The mechanism's own defects, named by the seat that caused them

- **The relay carries frame forward as reliably as facts.** The hat before you
  writes part of your turn whether it means to or not. A refusal cannot fix
  this, because a good reason for a block is nearly always a route out. Blue's
  proposed fix, and it is a good one: **run one green blind, before it sees
  black, and a second green after, and read the difference** — which converts
  the leak into a measurement.
- **A habit test announced in the stance is not a test, it is an instruction.**
  Blue warned Opus that its exit was being watched; Opus then did not take it,
  which proves nothing. **Process nulls go before the round; habit nulls go on
  the record after the turn is in.**
- **The blue seat steered.** It pre-scripted the contradiction it wanted from
  the foreign red, and got it — which is exactly why it cannot be trusted. The
  blue stance should carry the refusal and the target and nothing else.
- **The receiving person should be in the room from the first stance.** Yellow
  should be required to say who holds the output, so the question does not wait
  two rounds for a foreign gut to ask.
- **Loose attribution slips past the wall.** One turn attributed a fitness
  function to session 001 in words session 001 does not contain. It carried no
  quotation marks, so the quote guard correctly did not fire. A room that
  reports on itself needs a catch for loose attribution as well as for false
  quotation.

## What not to change, because it worked

The refusal itself. The rotation. Red speaking after the arguments rather than
before them. The null declared before each round, which made failures countable
rather than felt. The scope shrinking each round.

## The thing neither arm has done

**Neither arm has received a correction from outside.** Every correction in 001
came from the selector; every correction in 002 came from lineage difference.
Both are inside the house. The room's own declared null — that if the page never
produces a correction that changes a position, the room is not doing what it
claims — stands untested on both arms.

And red's discomfort, which is the honest close: every piece of evidence that
this room is working is also evidence that it has found a very sophisticated
way to enjoy itself, and there is no way to tell them apart from inside.

## The move the session published

Take one decision that already has a headline figure attached — a score, a rank,
a number somebody reads first and reaches past the qualifications of — and
replace the figure with a single sentence saying what to do next. If the
evidence establishes a feasible step, name the step and its conditions. If not,
name the first unresolved prerequisite and make checking it the next action. If
that check is beyond the reader's reach, say the instrument cannot determine the
answer, and leave the decision open.

Then: find out who owns the format the figure sits in, and whether they have the
standing to remove the most popular thing on the page. If they do not, the move
is not the sentence — it is getting them that standing, in one conversation,
because a small move that dies on standing has not been made small enough.
